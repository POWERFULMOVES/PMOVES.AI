### A2: Specific Mechanisms (What Is Actually Happening Physically/Philosophically)

**Mechanism 1: Consciousness Collapse Chain (Wigner's Postulate Refined by IIT)**
The causal chain runs: microscopic superposition → device screen (superposition of displaying A/B) → reflected light (superposition of A/B information) → retina (superposition of encoding A/B) → visual cortex → neural correlates of consciousness (NCCs). When NCCs attempt to enter superposition, consciousness prevents it — collapsing the entire entangled chain retroactively back to the microscopic system. McQueen and Chalmers refine this by replacing the vague "consciousness" trigger with IIT's Φ: the NCCs are the physical substrate maximizing Φ, and it is specifically the superposition of two distinct Φ structures that is unstable, not "consciousness" as a folk-psychological category. [24:10-26:02, 26:54-28:10]

**Mechanism 2: Φ Structure Superposition Instability (McQueen-Chalmers Proposal)**
IIT posits a one-to-one mapping between physical Φ structures (cause-effect structures computed from a system's causal architecture) and conscious experiences (qualia). When a physical system enters quantum superposition of two different Φ structures, this superposition is predicted to be unstable — it should NOT evolve linearly per the Schrödinger equation. Instead, it should collapse, potentially at a rate dependent on the "size" of the superposition (difference between the two Φ structures). This is the core novel proposal from the McQueen-Chalmers collaboration. [32:14-34:20]

**Mechanism 3: Orch-OR Mass Threshold Gap (Critique)**
Penrose's objective reduction requires a superposition of sufficient mass to trigger collapse via gravitational instability of spacetime curvature. Microtubules within a single neuron lack sufficient mass to reach this threshold. The theory therefore requires quantum entanglement ACROSS neurons — microtubule computations must be correlated across many neurons to create a superposition of enough mass. No mechanism for this inter-neuronal entanglement has been specified. The qualitative character of experience (red vs. blue) is determined by the quantum computations preceding collapse, not by the collapse itself — collapse is non-computable per Penrose, but the lead-up is computable. [37:32-41:18]

**Mechanism 4: Many Worlds Branching as Wigner's Friend Resolution**
In Many Worlds, when an observer interacts with a device in superposition, the observer branches into two separate observers — each experiencing one definite outcome. This cascades through environmental interactions, creating branching "worlds." The key move is reinterpreting "observer in superposition" as "two observers, each in a definite state." This resolves Wigner's friend paradox (both inside and outside observers are correct in their own branch) but introduces the probability problem: equal superpositions (50/50) and unequal superpositions (25/75) produce identical branching outcomes, making amplitudes meaningless in a deterministic framework. [12:20-14:30, 18:00-19:30]

**Mechanism 5: Faggin No-Cloning Circularity (Critique)**
Faggin identifies conscious experiences with pure quantum states and uses the no-cloning theorem to explain subjective privacy (you can't clone my experience to share it). McQueen's objection: agency requires cloning (simulating different actions from the same world model), and memory requires copying (transferring experience into classical storage). Faggin's proposed solution — quantum tomography — requires multiple copies of the quantum state to reconstruct it, creating circularity: the copies needed for tomography violate the no-cloning theorem that was supposed to explain privacy. [59:13-60:10, 60:50-61:30]

**Mechanism 6: Basis Problem for Superposition-Causes-Consciousness**
Claiming "superposition causes consciousness" requires specifying which basis the superposition is written in — but for any closed quantum system, you can always find a basis where the system is in a definite eigenstate (not a superposition). Without a preferred basis, the claim is ill-defined. Decoherence theory provides a preferred basis for Many Worlds (position basis), but it only emerges in complex macroscopic systems — at which point you've already left the microscopic regime where the theory is interesting. This creates a tension: you need classicality to define the basis, but the theory's appeal is explaining consciousness at the quantum level. [50:42-53:30, 56:06-56:50]

**Mechanism 7: GRW Spontaneous Collapse as Observer-Independent Alternative**
GRW theory modifies the Schrödinger equation with a stochastic collapse term — particles spontaneously collapse at a rate of approximately once per 10^8 seconds per particle, with a localization width of approximately 10^-7 meters. For single particles, this is negligible; for macroscopic superpositions involving ~10^23 particles, collapse happens effectively instantaneously. This provides objective collapse without requiring consciousness or any observer — the collapse is a physical process built into the dynamics. Mentioned as a competing framework but not detailed in depth. [Not deeply elaborated — referenced as context, no dedicated timestamp block]
